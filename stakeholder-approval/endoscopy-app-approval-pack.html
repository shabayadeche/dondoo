Executive Summary

What Will Be Built

The proposed product is a structured endoscopy reporting application that runs as both a website app and a mobile app from the same codebase. The primary documentation surface is desktop or tablet inside the unit; mobile extends the same product for quick review, draft completion, task follow-up, and sign-off.

Primary users
Endoscopists, nurses, unit admin
Pilot release
Colonoscopy MVP with shared workflow engine
Platforms

Why This Shape Is Recommended
  • Long clinical documentation is easier on desktop and tablet
  • Shared product logic keeps validation, reporting, and audit rules consistent
  • One backend prevents fragmented patient, case, and follow-up data
  • Mobile remains valuable without forcing clinicians into small-screen long-form entry
What Is Not Included In V1
  • Full EMR replacement
  • Billing and coding workflows
  • Deep external system integrations
  • ERCP and EUS modules in the first release

UX Direction

Experience Principles And Layout Strategy

The interface should feel calm, clinical, and efficient. The user experience is built around guided completion, progressive disclosure, visible save state, and low-friction sign-off.

Core UX Principles
  • Never present the entire procedure form at once
  • Use a stepper with explicit completion states
  • Highlight only the missing fields that block sign-off
  • Generate narrative documentation from structured inputs
  • Make draft save and final lock state visible at all times
Platform Behavior
  • Web and tablet prioritize complete procedure authoring
  • Mobile prioritizes quick review, tasks, and targeted edits
  • The same case, rules, and data should appear consistently across devices
  • Critical actions should use strong confirmations and audit logging

Governance And Security Controls
  • Every create, edit, finalize, and close action must be audit logged
  • Finalized reports must be locked and not silently editable
  • Each finalized report must preserve the template version used at sign-off
  • Role-based access should limit who can finalize, reopen, or administer templates
  • Specimen, pathology, and communication events should be time-stamped and attributable
Success Measures For The Pilot
  • More than 95% completion of mandatory fields on finalized reports
  • More than 90% same-day finalization for completed procedures
  • Median documentation completion time below five minutes after the procedure
  • All specimen-bearing cases linked to a follow-up state or closure record
  • Zero final record changes without audit trace
Operational Dependencies
  • Availability of devices in procedure and recovery areas
  • Agreement on local mandatory report content
  • Pilot users willing to validate real workflow timing
  • Defined owner for user administration and template governance
Main Delivery Risks
  • Scope creep into EMR, scheduling, or billing functions
  • Overloading mobile with desktop-style long-form entry
  • Weak analytics if critical fields are left as free text
  • Late sign-off rules causing rework in report generation and locking logic

Business Case

Why This Project Should Be Approved

The strongest reason to fund this product is operational: structured reporting reduces avoidable documentation variability, makes quality measures easier to retrieve, and creates a cleaner follow-up chain for pathology, communication, and surveillance planning.

Current-state risk
Inconsistent reports, missed fields, weak auditability
Operational gain
Faster reporting with more structured completeness
Strategic gain
Quality-ready data without parallel spreadsheet work
Problems This Investment Addresses
  • Procedure reports may vary significantly between users and sessions
  • Key safety and quality items may be omitted or buried in free text
  • Specimen and pathology follow-up may rely too heavily on informal reminders
  • Quality review often becomes manual because data is not captured in a reusable structure
  • Desktop and mobile continuity is weak when teams rely on disconnected tools
Value Stakeholders Can Expect
  • Cleaner and more consistent endoscopy documentation
  • Higher completion of mandatory fields before sign-off
  • Faster report finalization after procedures
  • Better visibility into follow-up ownership and closure status
  • A scalable foundation for later modules, dashboards, and integrations

Funding Logic

This proposal should be approved as a workflow, quality, and governance project rather than only as a form digitization effort. The long-term return comes from repeatable documentation, lower review friction, easier quality reporting, and safer closed-loop follow-up.

Colonoscopy Field Reference

Colonoscopy is the proposed first live module. This appendix page shows the full field set that should be represented in the MVP workflow, with repeatable lesion and follow-up structures visible to stakeholders.

Preparation, Insertion, And Completeness

  • Bowel preparation agent
  • Preparation quality: adequate or inadequate
  • BBPS by right, transverse, and left colon plus total score
  • Insertion time
  • Cecum reached: yes or no
  • Cecal landmarks: appendiceal orifice and ileocecal valve
  • Photo documentation for cecum and pathology
  • Terminal ileum status and withdrawal time
  • Technical limitation and adverse event

Segmental Examination And IBD Activity

  • Segment rows for terminal ileum, cecum, ascending colon, transverse colon, descending colon, sigmoid colon, and rectum or retroflexion
  • Each row captures normal, finding or inflammation or lesion, and photo status
  • Ulcerative colitis Mayo Endoscopic Score
  • Crohn disease SES-CD or other validated score
  • Disease extent

Polyp And Resection Recording

  • Repeatable polyp or lesion log rows with number, location, size in mm, morphology, resection method, complete resection, retrieved status, and container
  • Small polyp technique: cold snare, hot snare, forceps, or other

  • Pathology: none or pending tracking required
  • Screening or surveillance interval with pending pathology option
  • Reason for interval: guideline-based, prep quality, piecemeal resection, or other
  • Adverse event plan: routine discharge, observe or admit, or other
  • Communication: patient informed, referrer informed, written instructions given

Visual reference intent: colonoscopy should show clear separation between preparation and completeness, segmental inspection, repeatable polyp logging, and post-procedure follow-up decisions.

ERCP Field Reference

ERCP adds preprocedure risk mitigation, device management, fluoroscopy, and 30-day follow-up requirements. These fields should be visible to stakeholders now even though ERCP is planned after the initial MVP.

ERCP-Specific Preprocedure Checks

  • Papilla status and therapeutic intent
  • Imaging reviewed: ultrasound, CT, MRCP, EUS, prior ERCP
  • Antibiotic prophylaxis
  • Rectal NSAID choice or contraindication
  • Hydration plan
  • Pancreatic stent plan and rescue plan
  • Radiation protection verified
  • Pregnancy precautions addressed

Cannulation And Cholangiopancreatography

  • Bile duct cannulation success and technique
  • Cannulation difficulty: contacts and time
  • Unintended pancreatic duct access count
  • Pancreatic duct access details and prophylactic pancreatic duct stent
  • Cholangiogram details including CBD size, intrahepatic ducts, stones, strictures, leak, anatomy, and drainage achieved

Therapy And Radiation

  • Sphincterotomy type and details
  • Papillary dilation with balloon size
  • Stone therapy method
  • Stricture therapy including dilation, brush cytology, biopsy, or other
  • Biliary stent details including plastic or SEMS size and location
  • Other intervention
  • Fluoroscopy time, dose-area product, reference air kerma, images acquired, and dose-reduction measures

Outcome, Device Follow-Up, And 30-Day Review

  • Intended therapy and technical success
  • Stone clearance and biliary drainage status
  • Immediate adverse event
  • Temporary stent and removal or exchange due date
  • Pancreatic duct stent and migration or passage check
  • Patient contact, tracking register, repeat intervention, and timing
  • 30-day quality follow-up rows for post-ERCP pancreatitis, clinically significant bleeding, cholangitis or cholecystitis, and unplanned hospital visit or biliary intervention

Visual reference intent: ERCP should feel procedure-safety heavy, with explicit preventive steps, device detail, fluoroscopy capture, and longitudinal follow-up after the immediate report is complete.

Field Reference Appendix

EUS Field Reference

EUS combines anatomical examination, lesion characterization, staging, tissue acquisition, and interventional options. These groups should be visible in the future-state design even if the first release begins with a simpler procedure type.

Procedure Type And Anatomic Examination

  • Echoendoscope type: radial, linear, or miniprobe
  • EUS intent: diagnostic, tissue acquisition, or therapeutic
  • Upper GI anatomy reviewed
  • Pancreaticobiliary anatomy reviewed
  • Other anatomy including liver, adrenal, mediastinum, and nodes
  • Doppler used
  • Relevant anatomy documented and photos or images
  • Technical limitation and immediate adverse event

Lesion Characterization And Staging

  • Repeatable lesion or mass or node rows with site, size in mm, echogenicity, margins, vascular relation, nodes, and image number
  • Cancer site
  • Staging system: TNM or other
  • T stage, N stage, M features, vascular invasion, overall EUS stage, and management implication

Tissue Acquisition

  • Target and approach: transgastric, transduodenal, transesophageal, or other
  • Needle type: FNA or FNB
  • Gauge: 19G, 20G, 22G, 25G, or other
  • Needle design and number of passes
  • ROSE availability and adequacy
  • Macroscopic on-site evaluation
  • Specimen type and container
  • Adequacy at procedure, label verification, complication, and antibiotics

Interventional EUS And Follow-Up

  • Interventional procedure type including PFC drainage, biliary drainage, gallbladder drainage, gastroenterostomy, EDGE, or other
  • Target or route and device or stent
  • Technical success
  • Clinical plan and immediate adverse event
  • Impression
  • Pathology or cytology tracking
  • Follow-up imaging or procedure
  • Multidisciplinary referral and communication status

Visual reference intent: EUS should be grouped around diagnostic anatomy, lesion detail, acquisition workflow, and interventional follow-up so that the complexity remains understandable and navigable.
